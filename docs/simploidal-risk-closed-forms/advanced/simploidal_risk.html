Risk on an assembled simploidal set

The product formulas in Products of simplices extend to any finite simploidal set with compatible positive factor degrees. Shared faces are fitted once, and their errors enter every incident cell. Consequently, adding the risks of independently fitted maximal cells gives the wrong answer. The formula below uses the global proper controls of on_set(), including repeated controls caused by self-identifications and degenerate boundary faces.

This chapter proves the formula and optimal allocations implemented by skeleton_risk_coefficients(), optimal_skeleton_ratio(), sample_skeleton() and fit_skeleton_regression() for spaces constructed with on_set(). The single-cell constructors retain their factorized risk calculation and original sampling behavior.

The later analytic results eliminate assembled matrices for regular glued complexes, for arbitrary sets at linear factor degrees, and for every one-dimensional set at every positive degree. The regular case reduces to weighted face incidences; the other two cases explicitly retain the cross terms from identified corners. These are mathematical simplifications; the public assembled risk implementation still uses matrix propagation.

Using the assembled APIs

Two quadratic intervals with one shared endpoint illustrate the whole path:

import torch
from torch_bsf import BezierModel, BezierSpace, Samples, SimploidalSet
from torch_bsf.fitting import fit_skeleton_regression
from torch_bsf.sampling import (
    optimal_skeleton_ratio, sample_skeleton, skeleton_risk_coefficients,
)

domain = SimploidalSet()
left, right = domain.add_simplex(1), domain.add_simplex(1)
domain.identify(left.face(0, 0), right.face(0, 1))
space = BezierSpace.on_set(domain, degree=2)
print(tuple(round(a, 6) for a in skeleton_risk_coefficients(space)))
ratio = optimal_skeleton_ratio(space)
points = sample_skeleton(space, 120, ratio=ratio, seed=0)
truth = BezierModel(space, torch.arange(space.n_controls, dtype=torch.float64)[:, None])
result = fit_skeleton_regression(space, Samples(points, truth(points).detach())).require_success()
assert torch.allclose(result.model.coefficients, truth.coefficients)
assert torch.allclose(result.model(points), truth(points))
assert all(isinstance(cid, int) for cid in result.faces)
assert sum(face.diagnostics.n_samples for face in result.faces.values()) == 120

(0.75, 2.0)

The default evaluation measure gives equal weight to maximal cells. Specify evaluation_weights separately from the sampling allocation to evaluate another measure, including a mixture with lower-dimensional cells:

evaluation = {left: 3.0, right: 1.0}
c = skeleton_risk_coefficients(space, evaluation_weights=evaluation, allocation="cell")
q = optimal_skeleton_ratio(space, evaluation_weights=evaluation, allocation="cell")
points = sample_skeleton(space, 120, cell_weights=q, seed=0)
assert set(c) == set(q) == set(result.faces)
assert abs(sum(q.values()) - 1) < 1e-14
risk_factor = sum(c[cid] / q[cid] for cid in c)
assert abs(risk_factor - sum(value**0.5 for value in c.values())**2) < 1e-12

allocation="dimension" (the default) returns a tuple indexed by dimension, including zero entries for dimensions without active cells. It uses \(a_m=K_m\sum_{\dim s=m}c_s\) and assigns equal shares to the \(K_m\) active cells of each dimension. allocation="cell" returns an increasing-dimension, then increasing-cell-ID dictionary and optimizes the original cell objective. Cell mode needs addressable faces: use on_set for positive-dimensional spaces; a bare BezierSpace.simplex or simploid has only one chart ID and cannot name its faces this way. Zero-dimensional spaces support both modes.

evaluation_weights and cell_weights accept integer IDs or the original builder handles. An ID and its handle are aliases whose weights add. Missing keys have weight zero. Weights must be real, finite, nonnegative, with a finite positive total, and are normalized to sum to one. Real values are converted to float64 before accumulation; values outside its range and normalization that underflows a positive cell weight raise ValueError. Unknown cells, booleans and invalid weights raise ValueError. cell_weights may give positive mass only to active cells and is mutually exclusive with ratio. Evaluation weights may include any compiled cell. They never change an explicit sampling allocation. Pass the same evaluation weights when requesting coefficients and an automatic ratio.

Sampling first rounds dimension budgets by largest remainder (dimension order breaks ties), then splits each budget equally among its active cells, randomly breaking remainder ties with a local generator. Explicit cell shares use one largest-remainder rounding in fitting order. Both paths return exactly n rows, including for n=0 or budgets smaller than the active-cell count. Positive shares on empty dimensions are rejected. Small budgets and rounded zero counts do not guarantee that fitting has enough observations or full rank. Within each cell, method="random" and method="sobol" retain the existing product-uniform exponential normalization; neither changes global random state. Reusing a seed reproduces coordinates, IDs and row order.

For fitting, a directly tagged lower-cell row and a boundary row expressed in an upper chart both reach their nondegenerate cell. Boundary coordinates must be exactly zero. Face restriction and degeneracy maps sum coordinates of repeated vertices and discard collapsed factors. Each input row is assigned once, and every shared cell is estimated once. The constrained solves retain the original chart coordinates, so membership-validation tolerances do not change the observed design when a nearly unit factor is collapsed. Cells without proper controls are ignored and counted in the result message. Result faces keys are integer cell IDs and each value has the ordinary fit diagnostics. The original single-cell coordinate-tuple keys are preserved. Missing positive-weight observations raise an error naming the cells; deficient rank remains a successful least-squares solve with explicit rank diagnostics. A solver failure stops assembly, identifies the failed cell, and leaves later stages absent and their proper controls zero.

A zero evaluation weight need not make a cell coefficient zero: boundary errors can propagate into evaluated cells. If a coefficient is zero, that cell still owns controls and needs positive sampling share for the theorem’s estimator. With a zero-coefficient active cell, the cell formula describes only an infimum over strictly positive allocations, so automatic cell allocation raises ValueError. Dimension allocation remains feasible when such a cell shares a dimension with a positive coefficient; the equal split still samples it. It raises when an entire active dimension has zero coefficient. Supply strictly positive explicit shares to estimate all active controls in either case. Explicit zero shares are allowed for sampling but do not promise that fitting all controls will succeed.

The assembled risk implementation uses float64 local moment/Cholesky solves and a unit triangular propagation solve. It retains repeated-control cross terms. It checks local Gram and propagation condition numbers, rejecting a first-order roundoff budget above 1e-6. Positive subnormal evaluation weights or weighted trace contributions are rejected as numerically unreliable; they are never reported as structural zeros or as a mathematical infimum. The first-order screen budgets moment/reduction errors of order \((2w+p+2)u\) across \(d+1\) propagation levels, multiplied by the largest local Gram condition and the squared propagation condition, with a safety factor of eight for whitening, trace rounding and higher orders. Here \(w\) is the maximum chart width, \(p\) the global control count and \(u=2^{-53}\). This conservative screen is not an all-degree certificate. Its conservative work limits are 512 global controls and a total of 4,000,000 local Gram entries. Dense propagation costs quadratic global memory and cubic global time, with extra local moment and trace contractions. Explicit ratio or cell_weights lets sampling proceed without this risk calculation; fitting uses the usual solver and its diagnostics. Positive-dimensional degree-zero factors remain unsupported. These risk formulas assume the correctly specified model, independent homoscedastic noise, independent product-uniform observations and unregularized least squares; Sobol sampling is a design option rather than a claim that the independent-design risk theorem applies to its dependence.

Global controls and moments

Let \(\mathcal S\) be the finite collection of non-degenerate cells, with maximal dimension \(d\). The set may be disconnected, have cells of different dimensions or types, and have identified or degenerate faces. Use a compatible degree assignment as in Single and assembled domains. For a cell \(s\) of type \((d_{s,1},\ldots,d_{s,k_s})\) and factor degrees \(D_{s,i}\geq1\), let \(T_s\) be its proper controls, namely the local Bernstein indices that are strictly positive in every coordinate. They are distinct global controls, and

\[p_s=|T_s|=\prod_i\binom{D_{s,i}-1}{d_{s,i}},\qquad p=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}p_s.\]

A binomial coefficient is zero when its lower index exceeds its upper index. A vertex has an empty product and one control. The sets \(T_s\) partition the \(p\) global proper controls. Call a cell active if \(p_s>0\), and write \(\mathcal A\) for the active cells. Degenerate cells add no controls or fitting stages: their coefficients are pulled back from their non-degenerate base.

Let \(\beta_s(t)\) be the column vector of the full local product Bernstein basis. Define the local-to-global matrix \(A_s\) by

\[\begin{split}(A_s)_{\alpha j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{local control }\alpha \text{ is global control }j,\\0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases} \qquad \phi_s(t)=A_s^\top\beta_s(t).\end{split}\]

Each row of \(A_s\) has one 1, but a column may have several. Thus the corresponding entries of \(\beta_s\) must be summed. This includes multiplicities from every face map; replacing them by a set of distinct control IDs would lose cross terms.

Use the product uniform probability measure \(U_s\) on each chart. The local and assembled Gram matrices are

(25)\[Q_s=\int\beta_s\beta_s^\top\,dU_s,\qquad G_s=A_s^\top Q_s A_s=\int\phi_s\phi_s^\top\,dU_s.\]

Every entry follows directly from Dirichlet moments:

\[(Q_s)_{\alpha\gamma} =\prod_i\frac{(D_{s,i}!)^2d_{s,i}! \prod_j(\alpha_{ij}+\gamma_{ij})!} {(2D_{s,i}+d_{s,i})! \prod_j\alpha_{ij}!\gamma_{ij}!}.\]

For risk evaluation, choose fixed weights \(w_s\geq0\) summing to one, and put \(\mu=\sum_s w_sU_s\), interpreting each chart measure on the identified domain. Then

\[G_\mu=\sum_s w_sG_s,\qquad R_N=E\sum_s w_s\int\|\hat b_s(t)-b_s(t)\|^2\,dU_s(t).\]

There is no canonical uniform probability measure across cells of different dimensions. Equal weights on maximal cells, geometric-volume weights, and weights on lower cells describe different risks. The evaluation weights are independent of the sampling fractions below.

Estimator and finite propagation formula

Assume the correctly specified model \(b_s(t)=\phi_s(t)^\top b\) with \(L\) outputs. On each active cell \(s\), take a deterministic number \(n_s\) of independent \(U_s\) samples, with independent draws across cells, including cells that share a face. All noise vectors are mutually independent and independent of all design rows, with mean zero and covariance \(\sigma^2I_L\), with \(\sigma>0\).

Visit active cells in increasing dimension. On \(s\), estimate only \(b_{T_s}\) by ordinary least squares, holding all proper controls on its boundary at their earlier estimates. Every nonzero entry of \(\phi_s\) belongs either to \(T_s\) or to these boundary controls, whose set of distinct global IDs is denoted by \(B_s\). No second fit is made for another occurrence of an identified face.

Set \(H_s=G_s[T_s,T_s]\). This block equals the local proper-control Gram block and is positive definite, because those local Bernstein polynomials are linearly independent. Order the global controls by their owning cell’s dimension, and define the \(p\times p\) matrix \(C\) by the blocks

(26)\[C[T_s,B_s]=H_s^{-1}G_s[T_s,B_s],\qquad C[T_s,j]=0\quad(j\notin B_s),\qquad P=(I+C)^{-1}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}(-C)^\ell.\]

Indeed, every nonzero block has a strictly higher row dimension than column dimension, so \(C^{d+1}=0\). No inverse of a global Gram matrix is needed. Write \(E_s=P[:,T_s]\) and define the nonnegative cell coefficients

(27)\[c_s(\mu)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(H_s^{-1}E_s^\top G_\mu E_s\bigr).\]

Equations (25)–(27) are a finite expression for every compatible degree and finite simploidal set. They require only local proper-block solves and a finite polynomial in \(C\). Rational evaluation weights give rational coefficients. In general the matrices do not factor globally over the simplex factors: the identifications and the evaluation measure are part of the answer.

Theorem 19 (Assembled asymptotic risk)

With the estimator and assumptions above, let \(N=\sum_{s\in\mathcal A}n_s\) and suppose \(n_s/N\to q_s>0\) for every active cell. Then \(R_N\) is finite for all sufficiently large \(N\), and

(28)\[\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{NR_N}{\sigma^2L} =\sum_{s\in\mathcal A}\frac{c_s(\mu)}{q_s}.\]

Proof. Let \(Z_{s,T}\) and \(Z_{s,B}\) be the design blocks on cell \(s\). With empirical moments denoted by hats, its exact error equation is

\[e_{T_s}=\eta_s-\hat H_s^{-1}\hat G_s[T_s,B_s]e_{B_s},\qquad \eta_s=(Z_{s,T}^\top Z_{s,T})^{-1}Z_{s,T}^\top\varepsilon_s.\]

Hence \(e=\hat P\eta=\sum_s\hat E_s\eta_s\), using the same finite polynomial as (26). Conditional on all design rows, the innovations are independent, and each output column of \(\eta_s\) has covariance \(\sigma^2\hat H_s^{-1}/n_s\). Therefore, exactly,

\[\frac{NR_N}{\sigma^2L}=E X_N,\qquad X_N=\sum_s\frac{N}{n_s} \operatorname{tr}(\hat H_s^{-1}\hat E_s^\top G_\mu\hat E_s).\]

The strong law on each of the finitely many charts gives \(\hat H_s\to H_s\), \(\hat C\to C\) and \(\hat P\to P\) almost surely, proving the corresponding limit of \(X_N\).

For expectation convergence, each chart is convex in its affine span and its basis has bounded total polynomial degree \(\sum_iD_{s,i}\). Thus Lemma 2 applies on every positive-dimensional active cell. Vertices have the deterministic block \(H_s=1\). The assembled basis entries are sums of finitely many Bernstein polynomials and are bounded. The finite propagation polynomial bounds \(X_N\) by a fixed polynomial in the local inverse norms, since \(N/n_s\) is bounded. Hölder’s inequality and the inverse-moment lemma give a uniform second-moment bound, exactly as in Theorem 7. This proves finiteness and uniform integrability, and hence (28).

Optimal allocation and all-at-once comparison

If \(G_\mu\) is positive definite, every active \(c_s\) is positive: \(E_s[T_s]=I\), so it has full column rank. A sufficient condition is positive evaluation weight on every maximal cell. Each global control occurs in at least one maximal chart, and every local Bernstein basis is linearly independent, so their assembled Gram sum is positive definite.

Corollary 10 (Cell and grouped allocations)

When \(G_\mu\) is positive definite, the optimal individual-cell allocation and normalized risk are

\[q_s^*=\frac{\sqrt{c_s}}{\sum_t\sqrt{c_t}},\qquad C_{\rm cell}^*=\left(\sum_s\sqrt{c_s}\right)^2.\]

More generally, partition the active cells into groups \(\mathcal A_g\) and require an equal split within each group. With \(K_g=|\mathcal A_g|\) and total group fraction \(\rho_g\),

(29)\[a_g=K_g\sum_{s\in\mathcal A_g}c_s,\qquad C(\rho)=\sum_g\frac{a_g}{\rho_g},\qquad \rho_g^*=\frac{\sqrt{a_g}}{\sum_h\sqrt{a_h}},\qquad C_{\rm group}^*=\left(\sum_g\sqrt{a_g}\right)^2\geq C_{\rm cell}^*.\]

Proof. Substitute \(q_s=\rho_g/K_g\) in (28). Cauchy-Schwarz gives both minima as in Theorem 8. Individual-cell allocations include the group-constrained ones.

Grouping by \(\dim s=m\) gives the general skeleton coefficients \(a_m=K_m\sum_{\dim s=m}c_s\); an empty group has coefficient zero. There is generally no symmetry making all \(c_s\) in a group equal. For one unglued product cell and uniform evaluation on it, the propagation matrices reduce to Lemma 3; this recovers (6) and the face-shape optimum.

If \(G_\mu\) is only positive semidefinite, the risk theorem still requires strictly positive fractions on all active cells. For individual allocations, the square-root expression is an infimum approached by making the fractions of zero-\(c_s\) cells arbitrarily small and positive; it is attained when every \(c_s>0\). For equal-within-group allocations, only groups with \(a_g=0\) receive vanishing fractions in this limiting argument. If every nonempty group has \(a_g>0\), the grouped minimum is attained, even when some cells within a group have zero trace: those cells receive the same positive fraction as the other members of their group. Neither limiting prescription justifies leaving active cells unfitted, or a simultaneous limit with vanishing fractions. The estimator still needs their controls, even when the evaluation measure cannot see them.

Corollary 11 (Global all-at-once fitting)

Fit all \(p\) proper controls jointly, with deterministic chart counts \(n_s/N\to v_s>0\) on a fixed collection \(\mathcal D\) of non-degenerate cells. Suppose \(G_\nu=\sum_{s\in\mathcal D}v_sG_s\) is positive definite. With the same model and independent noise,

\[\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{NR_N}{\sigma^2L} =\operatorname{tr}(G_\nu^{-1}G_\mu).\]

In particular, matching the design and evaluation weights gives \(p\), the number of global proper controls, whenever \(G_\mu\) is positive definite.

Proof. Conditional covariance is \(\sigma^2(Z^\top Z)^{-1}\) per output, and \(Z^\top Z/N\to G_\nu\). To justify the expectation limit, apply the inverse-moment lemma to the full local Bernstein basis on each sampled chart. If \(\lambda_s\) is the smallest eigenvalue of its whitened empirical Gram matrix, then, for all sufficiently large \(N\),

\[Z^\top Z/N\succeq\tfrac12(\min_{s\in\mathcal D}\lambda_s)G_\nu.\]

The inverse moments of the minimum are bounded by sums of the finitely many local inverse moments. This gives uniform integrability; the trace limit follows. For \(G_\nu=G_\mu\), the trace is \(p\).

The deterministic chart counts matter for this statement. Randomly selecting chart labels can leave a whole connected component unobserved with positive probability at finite \(N\); invertibility then needs a separate argument.

Incidence formulas on regular complexes

The assembled propagation matrices can be eliminated on ordinary glued meshes. Here regular means that every local face of every chart maps isomorphically to a non-degenerate global cell, and distinct local faces of one chart map to distinct global cells. Different charts may share faces, with any number of incident charts; no manifold assumption is needed. Identifications within the closure of a single chart and collapsed faces are excluded from this particular result.

Theorem 20 (Risk from weighted face incidences)

Suppose the simploidal set is regular. For an active cell \(s\) which is a face of a chart \(x\), write its unique local face as \(F_{s,x}=\prod_i I_i\), and put \(M_i=d_{x,i}+1\), \(r_i=|I_i|-1\). Define

(30)\[\lambda_{s,x} =\prod_i\frac{a_{r_i}(M_i,D_{x,i})} {\binom{M_i}{r_i+1}^2},\qquad c_s(\mu)=\sum_{x:\,s\preceq x}w_x\lambda_{s,x}.\]

The empty product for a vertex chart is one. The simplex coefficients \(a_r(M,D)\) are those of Theorem 11. Consequently this formula uses finite scalar sums and face incidences, with no assembled matrix or Gram inverse. It allows different cell dimensions and types, compatible factor degrees, and evaluation weights on lower-dimensional charts as well as maximal ones.

Proof. Fix a source innovation in \(T_s\), setting all other innovations to zero. Its error is zero on every cell that does not contain \(s\): induction over dimension in the error equations proves this support statement. On a chart \(x\) containing \(s\), all its faces occur exactly once and are non-degenerate. Restricting the global recursion to these faces therefore gives exactly the normal equations of the unglued product chart, with source face \(F_{s,x}\). Positive definite proper-control blocks give the same unique extension. A reordering of source controls induced by a face identification also reorders \(H_s\), leaving the covariance trace unchanged.

The product face trace is \(\lambda_{s,x}\) by (3). Finally, the evaluation metric is a weighted sum of chart metrics, and the trace is linear in that metric. Summing the local contributions proves (30).

This is stronger than adding independently fitted cell risks: a shared source is sampled and fitted once, and its single coefficient adds all incident evaluation weights before the allocation is optimized.

Corollary 12 (The face vector of a pure simplicial complex)

Suppose a regular simplicial complex is pure of dimension \(M-1\), every simplex has the same degree \(D\), and evaluation is supported on its maximal simplices. Their weights may be arbitrary, summing to one. Let \(K_m\) be the number of global \(m\)-faces and \(f_m=\binom M{m+1}\). For active dimensions,

(31)\[\begin{split}d_w(s)&=\sum_{x:\,s\preceq x}w_x,\qquad c_s=\frac{a_m(M,D)}{f_m^2}d_w(s)\quad(\dim s=m),\\ A_m&=\frac{K_m}{f_m}a_m(M,D).\end{split}\]

Thus the dimension-grouped risk and its optimum depend only on the face counts and the single-simplex coefficients, and are independent of the distribution of evaluation weights among maximal simplices. Individual-cell optimal allocations still depend on \(d_w(s)\).

Proof. Every maximal chart has \(f_m\) local \(m\)-faces. Double counting gives \(\sum_{\dim s=m}d_w(s)=f_m\sum_xw_x=f_m\). Substitution into \(A_m=K_m\sum_{\dim s=m}c_s\) proves the result.

For example, an arbitrary pure regular triangular complex of degree two, with \(V\) vertices and \(E\) edges, has

(32)\[c_v=d_w(v)/24,\qquad c_e=d_w(e)/3,\qquad (A_0,A_1,A_2)=(V/8,E,0).\]

The triangle coefficients are \(a_0=3/8\), \(a_1=3\). There are \(p=V+E\) global controls. If the evaluation Gram matrix is positive definite, dimension-grouped skeleton fitting therefore beats matching all-at-once fitting precisely when

\[\left(\sqrt{V/8}+\sqrt E\right)^2<V+E \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E/V<49/32.\]

At \(E/V=49/32\) the two risks agree. This comparison concerns the dimension-grouped design; allowing separate cell allocations can improve its skeleton risk further.

Linear degrees on arbitrary identified sets

When every factor degree is one, all proper controls belong to vertices. There is then an explicit formula even with repeated faces and degeneracies. For a chart \(x\) put \(M_i=d_{x,i}+1\) and label its local corners by \(a\in\prod_i[M_i]\). Let \(\pi_x(a)\) be the global vertex of that corner, with repetitions retained, and write \(\mathcal F_{x,v}=\{a:\pi_x(a)=v\}\).

Theorem 21 (Corner collisions at linear factor degrees)

On any finite simploidal set with all factor degrees one, the vertex coefficient is

(33)\[c_v(\mu)=\sum_x \frac{w_x}{\prod_iM_i(M_i+1)} \sum_{a,b\in\mathcal F_{x,v}} 2^{|\{i:a_i=b_i\}|}.\]

Empty products and the single corner of a vertex chart have their usual values. With \(V\) global vertices, only dimension zero is active, so \(A_0=V\sum_vc_v\). The individually optimal normalized risk satisfies

(34)\[C_{\rm cell}^*= \left(\sum_v\sqrt{c_v}\right)^2 \leq A_0\leq V=p.\]

The first expression is interpreted as an infimum if some coefficients vanish. If \(G_\mu\) is positive definite, \(p\) is the matching all-at-once risk. Both skeleton designs have strictly smaller risk than \(p\) if some positive-weight chart has at least two distinct global corner vertices.

Proof. For a positive-dimensional cell at least one factor has \(\binom{1-1}{d_i}=0\), so it has no proper controls. Thus \(C=0\), \(H_v=1\), and the propagated vertex polynomial is just its assembled basis function \(\phi_{x,v}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal F_{x,v}}\prod_i t_{i,a_i}\). Uniform simplex moments give

\[E\,[t_{i,a_i}t_{i,b_i}] =\frac{1+\mathbf1_{a_i=b_i}}{M_i(M_i+1)}.\]

Squaring the assembled basis function and using independence of the factors proves (33), including every cross term between identified corners. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the first inequality in (34). The nonnegative assembled functions form a partition of unity on each chart; hence \(\sum_v\phi_{x,v}^2\leq1\), proving the second inequality after integration. If a positive-weight chart has two distinct corner vertices, both assembled functions are positive in its interior, so this last inequality is strict on a set of positive measure.

For a single unglued product, every corner fiber is a singleton and this recovers \(A_0=\prod_i2M_i/(M_i+1)\). At the other extreme, if all corners of every chart are the same single global vertex, the entire model is constant and \(c_v=A_0=C_{\rm cell}^*=1\).

Closed forms on arbitrary graphs

Every one-dimensional simploidal set is a finite multigraph, allowing loops, parallel edges and isolated vertices. Here edges mean the non-degenerate one-cells; a collapsed edge contributes through its base vertex. Assign each edge \(e\) a positive degree \(D_e\). Let \(w_v\) and \(w_e\) be the evaluation weights on vertices and edges, respectively, with their sum equal to one.

Theorem 22 (Graph risk coefficients)

Define

\[h_D=\frac{1}{D(D+2)},\qquad j_D=\frac{(-1)^{D-1}}{D(D+1)(D+2)},\qquad \ell_D=2(h_D+j_D) =\frac{2}{(D+1)(D+1+(-1)^D)}.\]

The assembled coefficients have the closed forms

(35)\[\begin{split}c_v=w_v+ \sum_{\substack{e\ni v\\e\text{ is not a loop}}}w_eh_{D_e} +\sum_{\substack{e\text{ is a loop}\\\text{at }v}}w_e\ell_{D_e}, \qquad c_e=w_e(D_e-1)\quad(D_e\geq2).\end{split}\]

Each ordinary incident edge occurs once in the first sum. Each loop occurs once in the second sum. Edges of degree one have no proper controls and no fitting stage, although their evaluation weights still contribute to the vertex coefficients.

Thus, under the assumptions of Theorem 19,

\[\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{NR_N}{\sigma^2L} =\sum_v\frac{c_v}{q_v} +\sum_{e:D_e\geq2}\frac{w_e(D_e-1)}{q_e}.\]

Whenever all active coefficients are positive, the individual-cell optimum is the square of the sum of their square roots. The general qualifications for zero coefficients remain those in Corollary 10.

Proof. On an edge of degree \(D\), write the endpoint propagation polynomials as \(\varphi_1(x)=\varphi(x)\) and \(\varphi_0(x)=\varphi(1-x)\), where \(\varphi\) is given in Theorem 15. Set

\[A=\frac{\widetilde P_D'}{D(D+1)},\qquad B=\frac{\widetilde P_{D+1}'}{(D+1)(D+2)}.\]

Then \(\varphi_1=(A+B)/2\) and \(\varphi_0=(-1)^{D-1}(A-B)/2\). The opposite parities make \(A\) and \(B\) orthogonal. Their squared norms are \(2/[D(D+1)]\) and \(2/[(D+1)(D+2)]\), respectively. Consequently,

\[\lVert\varphi_0\rVert^2=\lVert\varphi_1\rVert^2=h_D,\qquad \langle\varphi_0,\varphi_1\rangle=j_D.\]

An ordinary edge carries independent innovations at its two distinct vertices. A loop carries the same innovation at both endpoint occurrences, giving squared norm \(\lVert\varphi_0+\varphi_1\rVert^2=\ell_D\). The edge-interior innovation has trace \(D-1\), and there are no higher-dimensional cells to receive it. Weighting these contributions by the evaluation measure proves the formulas.

The loop coefficient has an exact parity pattern:

\[\ell_1=1,\qquad \ell_{2k}=\ell_{2k+1} =\frac{1}{(k+1)(2k+1)}\quad(k\geq1).\]

For positive degrees the reciprocal sequence is \(1,6,6,15,15,28,28,\ldots\). Its distinct values are the hexagonal numbers, identified in OEIS A000384. The identity above proves the pattern independently of the sequence identification.

Corollary 13 (Graph allocations by dimension)

Suppose the graph is loop-free, has \(V\) vertices and \(E\) edges, has no isolated vertices, and every edge has the same degree \(D\geq2\). Evaluate only on edges, with positive weights summing to one. Equal sampling shares within each dimension give

\[a_0=\frac{2V}{D(D+2)},\qquad a_1=E(D-1),\qquad C_{\rm dimension}^* =\left(\sqrt{\frac{2V}{D(D+2)}}+ \sqrt{E(D-1)}\right)^2.\]

These grouped coefficients do not depend on the edge weights or connectivity. With \(K=D(D+2)\), this optimum is smaller than the matching all-at-once risk \(p=V+E(D-1)\) exactly when

\[\frac EV<\frac{(K-2)^2}{8K(D-1)}.\]

Proof. Every edge contributes twice to the sum of vertex coefficients, so \(\sum_v c_v=2/K\), while \(\sum_e c_e=D-1\). Apply the grouped allocation formula. Since \(p-a_0-a_1=V(1-2/K)>0\), squaring the equivalent inequality \(2\sqrt{a_0a_1}<p-a_0-a_1\) gives the stated threshold.

For the individual optimum, the incidence pattern still matters. Writing \(\delta_v=\sum_{e\ni v}w_e\), the same loop-free setting gives

\[C_{\rm cell}^* =\left( \frac{\sum_v\sqrt{\delta_v}}{\sqrt{D(D+2)}}+ \sqrt{D-1}\sum_e\sqrt{w_e} \right)^2.\]

For example, two quadratic segments meeting at one endpoint, evaluated equally, have

\[C_{\rm cell}^*=\frac{27}{8}+\frac{5}{2\sqrt2}>5,\qquad C_{\rm dimension}^*=\frac{11}{4}+\sqrt6>5.\]

Thus even the individual optimum loses to the matching all-at-once risk \(p=5\) on this graph, although a single quadratic segment wins.

Corollary 14 (Complete comparison for a single loop)

For a single degree-\(D\) loop, evaluated uniformly on its edge,

\[C_{\rm loop}^*=(\sqrt{\ell_D}+\sqrt{D-1})^2,\qquad p=D.\]

The two risks are equal at \(D=1\). Inductive fitting loses at \(D=3,5\), and wins at \(D=2,4\) and every \(D\geq6\).

Proof. For \(D>1\), put \(T=1/\ell_D>1\). The sign of \(p-C_{\rm loop}^*\) is the sign of

\[\Delta=(T-1)^2-4(D-1)T.\]

For \(D=2k\), \(k\geq1\),

\[T=(k+1)(2k+1),\qquad \Delta=(2k^2-k-2)^2>0.\]

The quadratic inside the square has discriminant 17, so it has no integer root. For \(D=2k+1\), \(k\geq1\),

\[\Delta=k(4k^3-4k^2-15k-8).\]

This is negative for \(k=1,2\). For \(k=u+3\), \(u\geq0\), the cubic factor becomes \(4u^3+32u^2+69u+19>0\). At degree one the loop has only one constant global control, and both normalized risks equal one.

Two small examples

Take two quadratic segments meeting at one endpoint, and evaluate equally on the two segments. A single quadratic segment has vertex propagation polynomials \(f_0(t)=(1-t)^2-\tfrac32t(1-t)\) and \(f_1(t)=t^2-\tfrac32t(1-t)\), each with squared integral \(1/8\). The shared vertex propagates onto both segments. Consequently the three vertex traces are \(1/16,1/8,1/16\), while each segment-interior trace is \(1/2\). Thus

\[a_0=3/4,\qquad a_1=2,\qquad C_{\rm cell}^*=\left(\tfrac12+\tfrac{5}{2\sqrt2}\right)^2 <\left(\sqrt{3/4}+\sqrt2\right)^2=C_{\rm dimension}^*,\]

and matching all-at-once fitting has normalized risk \(p=5\). The optimal shared-vertex fraction is \(\sqrt2\) times either outer-vertex fraction, despite all three cells having dimension zero.

For a single quadratic segment with its two endpoints identified, both endpoint occurrences carry the same innovation. Its vertex propagation is \(f_0+f_1=1-5t(1-t)\), whose squared integral is \(1/6\). The coefficients are therefore \((a_0,a_1)=(1/6,1)\) and the all-at-once count is \(p=2\). Treating the endpoint occurrences as independent would instead give \(1/4\) for their combined vertex trace and miss the cross term. At degree one this loop has only one global control, \(\phi=1\), so its normalized risk is exactly \(1\).

Exact checks of these examples, mixed triangle-square domains, shared triangles, a torus and a sphere with collapsed edges are recorded in Exact verification of the skeleton risk.
