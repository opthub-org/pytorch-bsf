A classical leading constant for seven vertices

Keep the uniform-simplex assumptions of Theory of the skeleton risk. This chapter evaluates the next constant from Vertex coefficients at every fixed vertex count. It extends the finite harmonic telescope of A classical closed form for the codimension-four constant to weight five.

Theorem 45 (The sixth constant)

\[c_6=600\zeta(2)+2520\zeta(3)+13980\zeta(4) -4320\zeta(2)\zeta(3)-10080\zeta(5)>0.\]

Consequently, for the fixed seven-vertex simplex,

\[a_0(7,D)\sim\frac{C_7}{D^8},\qquad C_7=2205c_6.\]

This is a leading constant as the degree tends to infinity. It is neither a finite-degree error estimate nor an all-degree comparison.

Proof. Put \(H=H_{n-1}\), \(G=H_{n-1}^{(2)}\) and \(J=H_{n-1}^{(3)}\), with all three zero at \(n=1\). The existing elementary-symmetric coefficient formula gives

\[\begin{split}\phi_1(n-1)&=\frac1n,\\ \phi_2(n-1)&=\frac{2(1-H)}{n(n+1)},\\ \phi_3(n-1)&=\frac{6(H^2-G-3H+1)}{n(n+1)(n+2)},\\ \phi_4(n-1)&=\frac{12P(H,G,J)}{n(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)},\\ P(H,G,J)&=-2H^3+6HG-4J+11H^2-11G-12H+2.\end{split}\]

Thus \(e_5(n-1)=10\phi_1\phi_4+20\phi_2\phi_3\), and \(t_n=e_5(n-1)/120\) is

\[t_n=\frac{P(H,G,J)}{n^2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)} +\frac{2(1-H)(H^2-G-3H+1)}{n^2(n+1)^2(n+2)}.\]

Define \(U(n,H,G,J)=V(n,H,G,J)/(36n^2(n+1)(n+2))\), where

\[\begin{split}V={}&-(120n^3+420n^2+440n+144)H^3\\ &+(144n^3+504n^2+528n+144)HG -(24n^3+84n^2+88n)J\\ &+(-78n^3-183n^2+54n+144)H^2\\ &+(42n^3+111n^2+18n)G -(30n^3+84n^2+72n)H.\end{split}\]

Direct rational simplification proves the finite identity

(89)\[\begin{split}t_n={}&U(n+1,H+1/n,G+1/n^2,J+1/n^3)-U(n,H,G,J)\\ &+\frac{-20H^3+24HG-4J+47H^2-17G+21H+5}{6n^2}.\end{split}\]

This is an identity in the independent variables \(n,H,G,J\): after multiplying by \(36n^3(n+1)^2(n+2)(n+3)\), its numerator has every polynomial coefficient zero. Hence it holds at every positive integer n; it is not inferred from sampled harmonic values.

The first boundary is \(U(1,0,0,0)=0\). The other boundary tends to zero, because \(H_{n-1}\leq1+\log n\), \(G,J\leq2\) and \(U=O((1+\log n)^3/n)\). The displayed residual series converges absolutely by comparison with \((1+\log n)^3/n^2\). The original e5 series is absolutely convergent by the general coefficient bound. Summing the finite identity and only then taking its limit is therefore justified. No divergent harmonic series is assigned a value.

Use descending indices \(\zeta(k_1,\ldots,k_r)=\sum_{n_1>\cdots>n_r\geq1} n_1^{-k_1}\cdots n_r^{-k_r}\). All values below have \(k_1=2\), hence converge. Finite ordered-index decompositions give

\[\begin{split}\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{H^3}{n^2} &=6\zeta(2,1,1,1)+3\zeta(2,1,2) +3\zeta(2,2,1)+\zeta(2,3),\\ \sum_{n\geq1}\frac{HG}{n^2} &=\zeta(2,1,2)+\zeta(2,2,1)+\zeta(2,3),\\ \sum_{n\geq1}\frac{J}{n^2}&=\zeta(2,3),\\ \sum_{n\geq1}\frac{H^2}{n^2} &=2\zeta(2,1,1)+\zeta(2,2),\\ \sum_{n\geq1}\frac{G}{n^2}&=\zeta(2,2),\qquad \sum_{n\geq1}\frac{H}{n^2}=\zeta(2,1).\end{split}\]

Since \(c_6=6\sum e_5(j)=720\sum t_n\), this proves the finite convergent MZV expression

\[\begin{split}c_6=120\bigl(&-120\zeta(2,1,1,1)-36\zeta(2,1,2) -36\zeta(2,2,1)\\ &+94\zeta(2,1,1)+30\zeta(2,2) +21\zeta(2,1)+5\zeta(2)\bigr).\end{split}\]

The iterated-integral duality proved in A classical closed form for the codimension-four constant reverses the binary word and interchanges 0 and 1. Here it sends 01111 to 00001, 01101 to 01001, and 01011 to 00101, proving respectively

\[\zeta(2,1,1,1)=\zeta(5),\quad \zeta(2,1,2)=\zeta(2,3),\quad \zeta(2,2,1)=\zeta(3,2).\]

Splitting the absolutely convergent product into the cases where its two summation indices are greater, smaller or equal gives \(\zeta(2,3)+\zeta(3,2)=\zeta(2)\zeta(3)-\zeta(5)\). The same earlier chapter proves \(\zeta(2,1,1)=\zeta(4)\), \(\zeta(2,1)=\zeta(3)\) and \(\zeta(2,2)=3\zeta(4)/4\). Substitution proves the displayed classical formula. These are finite identities among convergent sums and integrals; no numerical integer-relation fit is used.

The original positive integral proves \(c_6>0\), independently of cancellation in the final expression. Substituting q=6 in the existing normalization gives \(C_7=7^2\,6!\,2^{-4}c_6=2205c_6\), proving the consequence.

Rational evaluation and saved records

The evaluator reuses the positive Euler-transformed ordinary zeta intervals proved in A classical closed form for the codimension-four constant. Their tail after N terms is at most \(2^{-N}/(1-2^{1-k})\) for order k. All orders needed here are fixed between 2 and 5. The rational-operation count is linear in N, while the integer bit lengths grow.

For positive intervals \(l_i\leq\zeta(i)\leq u_i\), the product is between \(l_2l_3\) and \(u_2u_3\). The coefficient -4320 therefore reverses those endpoints, as does the coefficient -10080 of zeta(5). The linear positive terms use their ordinary endpoints. The old positive-integral theorem permits clamping the lower c6 endpoint to zero. No floating-point approximation is required.

$ .venv/bin/python -m tools.risk_research.c6_closed_form --terms 64 --output .research/skeleton-risk/c6.json
$ .venv/bin/python -m tools.risk_research.c6_closed_form --verify .research/skeleton-risk/c6.json

The new record contains exact rational c6/C7 intervals, the finite classical and descending-index MZV coefficients, saved terms and source hashes. Verification restores nondefault terms and compares the complete strictly typed record. Source hashes normalize CRLF to LF; raw bytes still guard against concurrent source changes. Failed generation or verification preserves existing output. Protected evaluator/proof paths cannot be overwritten. Output uses a checked canonical parent and original leaf in a directory controlled by the caller, not a sandbox against another process replacing that directory inode.

The finite identity and source-independent elementary-symmetric rows are checked exactly. After freezing the theorem, independent finite checks compare the original general-vertex rational series and original positive Arb integral with this new expression. These checks validate implementation and normalization; the finite telescope and convergent identities above supply the proof. This chapter does not assert an arbitrary-q classical reduction, an optimal MZV basis, or M7’s all-degree comparison classification.
