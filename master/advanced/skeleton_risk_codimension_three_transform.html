A fast exact transform for codimension three

The positive double sum in Positive diagonal formulas for nonvertex risk can be evaluated by a single homogeneous polynomial transform and one angular sum. This chapter keeps its uniform-simplex design, evaluation and noise assumptions. It applies to every nonvertex source size \(s\geq2\), including the five-vertex edge and six-vertex triangle. It improves the exact arithmetic operation count; it does not claim a fixed-dimensional degree state, a minimal recurrence, or a bounded-length classical special-function expression.

The coefficient identity

For \(D\geq s\), put \(N=D-s\) and \(T=2D+s-1=2N+3s-1\). Use the explicit positive harmonic diagonals \(\delta_3(j)\) in (52) and define

\[\begin{split}F_N(u)&=\sum_{j=0}^N c_ju^j,\qquad c_j=\binom T{2j}\binom{2j}j\delta_3(j),\\ P_{s,D}(z)&=(1+z)^T F_N\!\left(\frac z{(1+z)^2}\right),\qquad B_L=[z^L]P_{s,D}(z).\end{split}\]

Despite its rational presentation, \(P_{s,D}\) is a polynomial: every exponent \(T-2j\) is at least \(3s-1>0\).

Theorem 47 (A homogeneous transform for all angular modes)

For every integer \(s\geq2,D\geq s\) and \(0\leq L\leq N\),

(91)\[\frac{B_L}{\binom TL^2} =\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^L \binom Lj\binom{T-L}j\delta_3(j)}{\binom TL}.\]

Consequently the exact coefficient is

(92)\[a_{s-1}(s+3,D)= \binom{s+3}s^2\frac{s(s+1)(s+2)} {(2D+s)(2D+s+1)(2D+s+2)} \sum_{L=0}^N\binom{N-L+s-2}{s-2} \frac{B_L}{\binom TL^2}.\]

The coefficient is zero for the inactive positive degrees \(D<s\).

Proof. For \(0\leq j\leq L\leq N\), comparison of factorials gives

\[\binom TL\binom Lj\binom{T-L}j =\binom T{2j}\binom{2j}j\binom{T-2j}{L-j}.\]

Expanding \(z^j(1+z)^{T-2j}\) shows that \(B_L\) is the sum of the right side times \(\delta_3(j)\). Terms with \(j>L\) cannot contribute. Division by \(\binom TL^2\) proves (91). In the existing positive nonvertex formula reindex the angular degree as \(k=N-L\). Its other upper binomial index is \(T-L\), its normalizer is \(\binom TL\), and its multiplicity is \(\binom{N-L+s-2}{s-2}\). This gives (92). All binomials and denominators are positive on this domain, including \(L=N=0\). The inactive prefix contains no source bubbles and is returned separately.

The final sum has \(N+1\) terms. Writing a polynomial coefficient does not by itself shorten its evaluation; the following algorithm is part of the construction.

Divide and conquer over integers

Set \(b(z)=(1+z)^2\). For a coefficient list \(C=(C_0,\ldots,C_{n-1})\), define

\[R(C)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} C_j z^j b(z)^{n-1-j}.\]

For \(n\geq2\), split after \(m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) entries into \(C^{\rm lo},C^{\rm hi}\). Direct separation of the terms gives

(93)\[R(C)=b^{n-m}R(C^{\rm lo})+z^mR(C^{\rm hi}),\qquad R((C_0))=C_0.\]

The powers \(b^r=(1+z)^{2r}\) are cached by their integer exponent. At a given recursion level there are at most two distinct list lengths, so all cached powers together have \(O(n)\) coefficients.

The implementation first sets \(Q=\operatorname{lcm}_{0\leq j\leq N} \operatorname{denominator}(\delta_3(j))\) and uses the integers \(C_j=Qc_j\). It evaluates (93) and then multiplies by \((1+z)^{3s-1}\). The first \(N+1\) coefficients are exactly \(QB_L\). The required input binomial weights have the nonsingular update

\[w_0=1,\qquad w_{j+1}=w_j\frac{(T-2j)(T-2j-1)}{(j+1)^2},\qquad w_j=\binom T{2j}\binom{2j}j.\]

Each quotient is an integer; its numerator is formed before exact division. Harmonic diagonals and the normalizers \(\binom TL\) also use their established nonsingular updates.

For fixed \(s\), the old triangular sum has exactly \((N+1)(N+2)/2\) rational-weight terms. If dense degree-\(n\) integer multiplication costs \(\mathsf M(n)\), the homogeneous evaluation uses \(O(\mathsf M(N+1)\log(N+2))\) integer operations plus \(O(N+1)\) fraction operations. The supplied balanced Karatsuba multiplier gives the stronger bound \(O((N+1)^{\log_2 3})\) integer operations for the transform, since both recursive levels have exponent strictly larger than one. This is a subquadratic arithmetic count, not a constant number of operations. The arrays and the cached powers contain \(O(N+1)\) integer entries at peak for fixed \(s\); integer storage in bytes grows with precision.

Bit lengths are not constant. The displayed harmonic formula shows that every diagonal denominator divides \(((N+3)!)^2\). Thus \(Q\) has \(O(N\log(N+2))\) bits. The transform weights and binomial coefficients add \(O(T)\) bits. Finally every angular denominator divides \(Q(T!)^2\), so the sum can be accumulated with integer bit lengths \(O(D\log(D+2))\) at fixed \(s\). Karatsuba uses subtraction internally, but only on integers; no floating-point cancellation or precision choice enters the algorithm. The mathematical coefficient identity retains positive weights and the slower positive double sum remains available as a separate reference.

Reproduction and limits

tools.risk_research.codimension_three_transform is an optional standard-library checkout tool. mode_averages(s,D) returns its averages in increasing radial length \(L\), and coefficient(s,D) returns the exact fraction. The two first target families use \(s=2\) and \(s=3\); the proof and implementation allow every \(s\geq2\). No sampling or production fitting API is changed.

$ .venv/bin/python -m tools.risk_research.codimension_three_transform --face-size 2 --degree 128 --output .research/skeleton-risk/q3-edge-transform.json
$ .venv/bin/python -m tools.risk_research.codimension_three_transform --face-size 3 --degree 128 --output .research/skeleton-risk/q3-triangle-transform.json
$ .venv/bin/python -m tools.risk_research.codimension_three_transform --face-size 2 --degree 128 --verify .research/skeleton-risk/q3-edge-transform.json --output .research/skeleton-risk/q3-edge-transform-rechecked.json

Records contain canonical decimal fractions and fingerprints of the evaluator, its relevant source modules and this proof. They use CRLF-to-LF normalized hashes, while before/after source checks compare raw bytes. Verification recomputes the exact value and compares the complete JSON record with scalar types preserved. Duplicate keys, nonfinite numbers and changed fields are rejected. Failed calculation or verification preserves an existing output. The resolved output is checked against proof/evaluator sources and the saved input before calculation and again immediately before the atomic write. The writer uses the validated canonical parent with the original leaf name, so retargeting the original parent alias cannot redirect it. Decimal serialization does not change Python’s process-wide integer-string limit.

Tests use arbitrary diagonal basis vectors to check the transform’s coefficient identity, the pre-existing double sum to check complete mode arrays, and original scalar/Gram formulas to check risk normalization. Larger frozen original-kernel values and post-construction holdouts are additional finite checks. The all-degree statement follows from the factorial identity and divide-and-conquer proof, rather than those finite comparisons. No finite-degree asymptotic remainder or simultaneous parameter limit is asserted here.
