Dyadic enclosures for multiple zeta values

This chapter supplies a rational numerical evaluator for the finite expressions in Finite MZV constructions for leading constants. It retains the strict descending-index convention. It does not change the expression compiler, sampling assumptions, finite-degree risk, or production API.

A half-interval identity

For any positive index tuple \(v=(s_1,\ldots,s_d)\), including a first index equal to one, put

\[L_v(z)=\sum_{n_1>\cdots>n_d\geq1} \frac{z^{n_1}}{n_1^{s_1}\cdots n_d^{s_d}},\qquad L_\varnothing(z)=1.\]

Encode the tuple by the binary word \(w(v)=0^{s_1-1}1\cdots0^{s_d-1}1\). The word is read from the outermost to innermost integral. Write \(\omega_0(t)=dt/t\), \(\omega_1(t)=dt/(1-t)\). For a word ending in one, its integral over \(z>t_1>\cdots>t_W>0\) is \(L_v(z)\). This convention agrees with Definition 2.1, Lemma 2.2 and Proposition 2.3 on printed pages 45–47 of Bachmann’s lecture notes, version 24. The following proof also derives the representation used here.

Theorem 40 (Dyadic split for every admissible MZV)

Let \(v\) be any nonempty positive integer tuple with \(s_1\geq2\), and let \(w=w(v)\) have length \(W\). For each cut \(w=p_j r_j\), \(|p_j|=j\), let \(p_j^*\) be the reversed complement of \(p_j\). Decode each nonempty word ending in one back to its index tuple. Then

\[\zeta(v)=\sum_{j=0}^{W} L_{p_j^*}(1/2)L_{r_j}(1/2).\]

Empty factors denote one. Every nonempty factor is an absolutely convergent multiple polylogarithm at one half, even if its first index is one.

Proof. In \(|z|<1\), the defining series is absolutely convergent: ignoring all denominators bounds the coefficient at \(z^n\) by \(\binom{n-1}{d-1}\), whose generating function is \(z^d/(1-z)^d\). Termwise differentiation gives \(L_{a,u}'(z)=L_{a-1,u}(z)/z\) for \(a>1\). At \(a=1\), summing \(\sum_{n>m}z^{n-1}\) gives \(L_{1,u}'(z)=L_u(z)/(1-z)\), including empty \(u\). The zero initial value of every nonempty tuple proves the stated iterated-integral formula by induction on the binary word length. At \(z=1\), monotone convergence and the convergent MZV sum identify the integral with \(\zeta(v)\); convergence also follows from the harmonic majorant in Finite MZV constructions for leading constants.

Partition the integration simplex by the number \(j\) of its variables above \(1/2\). The boundaries where a variable equals \(1/2\) have measure zero. The integrand is nonnegative, so Tonelli and the already finite total integral justify this partition and factorization. The lower variables give \(L_{r_j}(1/2)\). Substitute \(u_i=1-t_{j+1-i}\) in the upper variables: their order reverses and each zero/one differential form becomes the other one. They therefore give \(L_{p_j^*}(1/2)\). The original word starts in zero and ends in one. Thus every nonempty reflected prefix and every nonempty suffix ends in one and has the required convergent series. This also covers both endpoint cuts.

A rational geometric tail

Theorem 41 (Polylogarithm tail at one half)

For every positive tuple \(v=(a,u)\) of depth \(d\geq1\) and every integer \(T\geq1\), define

\[\begin{split}P_T(v)&=\sum_{n=1}^T\frac{H_{n-1}(u)}{2^n n^a},\\ E_T(v)&=\frac{1}{2^T(T+1)^a} \sum_{k=0}^{\min(d-1,T)}\binom Tk.\end{split}\]

Here the harmonic word uses strict descending indices and the empty harmonic word is one. Then

\[P_T(v)\leq L_v(1/2) \leq\min\{1,P_T(v)+E_T(v)\}.\]

For the empty word use the exact interval \([1,1]\) and zero tail. For each fixed tuple, the error is a polynomial in \(T\) times \(2^{-T}\). No numerical logarithm or floating-point rounding enters this bound.

Proof. For each outer index \(n\), there are \(\binom{n-1}{d-1}\) strict inner chains. Their reciprocal products are at most one. In the omitted tail, \(n^{-a}\leq(T+1)^{-a}\). The exact binomial identity

\[\sum_{n>T}\frac{\binom{n-1}{d-1}}{2^n} =\frac1{2^T}\sum_{k=0}^{\min(d-1,T)}\binom Tk\]

now proves the error bound. To see the identity without assuming an infinite rearrangement, put its proposed right-hand side equal to \(Q_T\). Pascal’s formula gives \(Q_T-Q_{T+1}=2^{-T-1}\binom T{d-1}\). For fixed \(d\), \(Q_T\to0\); summing this difference to a finite upper endpoint and taking the limit proves the identity. The full counting sum is one, proving \(L_v(1/2)\leq1\). All omitted terms are positive.

Target widths and signed constants

For a finite rational expression \(X=c_\varnothing+\sum_v c_v\zeta(v)\), apply the dyadic identity to every nonconstant word. Let \([l_u,h_u]\subseteq[0,1]\) be the polylogarithm intervals. Products use \([l_ul_v,h_uh_v]\). Since

\[h_uh_v-l_ul_v =(h_u-l_u)h_v+l_u(h_v-l_v) \leq E_T(u)+E_T(v),\]

a computable upper bound for the final interval width is

\[B_T(X)=\sum_v|c_v|\sum_{j=0}^{|w(v)|} (E_T(p_j^*)+E_T(r_j)).\]

Signs select the proper endpoints of each MZV interval. In particular, the absolute coefficient sum, rather than the signed sum, controls cancellation. For every fixed finite expression, \(B_T(X)\to0\). Testing \(T=1,2,4,8,\ldots\) therefore terminates for every requested absolute width \(2^{-b}\), \(b\geq1\), if operational limits are removed. Every accepted implementation result has actual rational width at most its target. This is an absolute-width guarantee; no relative accuracy is inferred near zero.

The method applies to every admissible MZV, hence to every fixed \(c_q\), \(q\geq3\), from the existing compiler. It does not require a classical-zeta reduction or a minimal basis. Multiplication by \((q+1)^2q!/2^{q-2}\) gives the vertex leading-constant interval; its width is scaled by that same positive factor. The requested width in constant mode concerns \(c_q\), not the scaled vertex constant. No finite-degree risk error or joint-limit assertion follows from this numerical theorem.

Implementation and bounded resources

tools.risk_research.mzv_dyadic exposes fixed-cutoff polylog_enclosure and mzv_enclosure and target-width mzv_precision_enclosure and constant_enclosure. Its exact harmonic table is updated in decreasing depth, so every suffix is still \(H_{n-1}\) during a strict-chain update. Polylogarithm contributions are accumulated before that update. Equal suffixes are shared.

For the resulting set of nonempty suffixes \(S\), one cutoff uses \(O(T\sum_{v\in S}(|v|+\mathrm{wt}(v)))\) selected arithmetic units, plus word-splitting, cutoff selection and expression construction. There are at most \(W+1\) cuts for a word of weight \(W\); constructing all decoded cuts costs \(O((W+1)^2)\) entries. The implementation charges for these selected operations and rejects work beyond the requested limit (default 200,000, maximum 1,000,000). It checks predicted numerical table work before entering its loops. The work counter is not a CPU/RSS or bit-complexity bound, nor does it count every Python instruction. The mathematical arbitrary-precision termination theorem is distinct from this bounded implementation, which can refuse large indices, weights, precisions or cutoffs.

build_record saves a direct MZV; build_constant_record saves a compiled \(c_q\) expression and its vertex scaling. Both preserve all options, selected cutoff/work, exact endpoints/width, convention, and canonical source/proof hashes. verify_record restores the options and compares the whole strict JSON object. Raw source bytes are checked before and after each generation, including direct API calls; the independent CLI also checks around the builder. LF/CRLF canonical hashes are portable, while a raw change during a calculation is rejected. Generation cannot replace a bound evaluator or proof. All numerical functions require only the Python standard library.

These are analytical error and termination results. Finite tests, comparisons with the previous harmonic-tail evaluator, independent original constant series/integrals, and measured resource comparisons validate the implementation separately; they do not establish the all-index theorem or a universal wall-time speedup.
