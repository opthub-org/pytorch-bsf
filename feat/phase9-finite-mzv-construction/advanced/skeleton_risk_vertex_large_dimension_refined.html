A sharper elementary large-vertex guarantee

Keep the uniform-simplex assumptions and the leading term \(B_M\) from The vertex leading constant at large vertex count. The degree-limit constant at fixed \(M\) is \(C_M=M^2(M-1)!2^{3-M}c_{M-1}\). This chapter improves the explicit error and starting vertex count. It does not assert a second correction coefficient or a joint \(M,D\) limit.

Theorem 41 (Improved first-order large-vertex guarantee)

For every integer \(M\geq162\),

(83)\[\left|C_M/B_M-1\right|\leq\frac6{M-2}.\]

In particular, one percent is guaranteed at every integer \(M\geq602\). The previous sufficient start was \(2502\); the previous simple radius was \(25/(M-2)\) starting at 1026. These are sufficient thresholds, not the smallest possible ones.

Proof. A signed fourth-derivative bound on a larger interval. Set \(n=M-2\), \(L=\log2\), \(\alpha=(L-1/2)/(4L)\), and retain the exact definitions

\[h(x)=4\cos^2(x/2)\log(2\cos(x/2))+x\sin x,\quad f(x)=h(x)/(4L),\quad A(x)=\sec^2(x/2).\]

The positive series for \(L\) gives \(29/42<842/1215<L<7/10\), so \(2/29<\alpha<1/14\). For \(0\leq x\leq1\), \(\cos(x/2)\geq7/8\). Integrating \(v\leq\tan v\leq8v/7\) on \([0,x/2]\) gives \(-x^2/6\leq\log\cos(x/2)\leq-x^2/8\). Differentiation gives the exact identity

\[h^{(4)}(x)=x\sin x+2\cos x\log(2\cos(x/2)) +\frac{\cos x}{1+\cos x}-2.\]

For its lower bound, use \(x\sin x\geq x^2-x^4/6\), \(\cos x\geq1-x^2/2\), \(\log(2\cos(x/2))\geq2/3-x^2/6\) and \(\cos x/(1+\cos x)\geq1/3\). The first two terms sum to at least \(4/3\); hence \(h^{(4)}\geq-1/3\). For the upper bound, the alternating sine and cosine estimates give

\[\begin{split}h^{(4)}(x) &\leq x^2-x^4/6+x^6/120 +2(1-x^2/2+x^4/24)(7/10-x^2/8)\\ &\qquad+1/2-x^2/8+x^4/96-2\\ &=-1/10-3x^2/40+13x^4/480-x^6/480<0.\end{split}\]

Here \(\cos x/(1+\cos x) =1/2-(1-\cos x)/(2(1+\cos x)) \leq1/2-x^2/8+x^4/96\). Since \(h\) is even, Taylor’s theorem and \(L>2/3\) therefore imply

\[1-\alpha x^2-x^4/192\leq f(x)\leq1-\alpha x^2.\]

Put \(u=1-f(x)\). The bound \(1/14+1/192<1/13\) proves \(0\leq u\leq x^2/13\). Thus \(f>0\) and

\[\log f(x)\leq-\alpha x^2,\qquad |\log f(x)+\alpha x^2| \leq\left(\frac1{192}+\frac1{312}\right)x^4 =\frac7{832}x^4.\]

This uses \(0\leq-\log(1-u)-u\leq u^2/(2(1-u))\). Also \(1\leq A\leq64/49<4/3\) and \(A-1=\tan^2(x/2)\leq16x^2/49<x^2/3\).

Gaussian moments with the same exponent. Let \(G_n=\sqrt\pi/(2\sqrt{\alpha n})\). The signed logarithmic estimate lets both exponentials use \(e^{-\alpha nx^2}\):

\[|f(x)^nA(x)-e^{-\alpha nx^2}| \leq\left(\frac43\frac7{832}nx^4+\frac{x^2}3\right) e^{-\alpha nx^2}.\]

Extend the positive majorant to infinity and use the second and fourth Gaussian moments. After division by \(G_n\), the error on \([0,1]\) is at most

\[\frac1n\left(\frac7{832\alpha^2}+\frac1{6\alpha}\right) <\frac1n\left(\frac{5887}{3328}+\frac{29}{12}\right) <\frac{17}{4n}.\]

The singular endpoint remains controlled. Monotonicity of \(f\) and the exact second moment \(I_2=\pi^3/(12L^2)<12\) are proved in the earlier chapter. They apply to \(I_n=\int_0^\pi f(x)^nA(x)\,dx\) for all \(n\geq2\). Since \(f(1)\leq1-\alpha<15/16\) and \(G_n>3/\sqrt n\),

\[\frac1{G_n}\int_1^\pi f(x)^nA(x)\,dx \leq4\sqrt n(15/16)^{n-2},\qquad \frac1{G_n}\int_1^\infty e^{-\alpha nx^2}\,dx \leq\frac8{3\sqrt n}e^{-n/16}.\]

Each right side is at most \(1/(2n)\) for all real \(n\geq160\). For the first, logarithmic differentiation shows that \(n^{3/2}e^{-(n-2)/16}\) decreases after 24. At 160, \(e^{158/16}>2^{14}>8\,160^{3/2}\): use \(14L<49/5<79/8\) and \(\sqrt{160}<127/10\), giving \(8\,160^{3/2}<16256<16384\). The second follows because \(\sqrt n e^{-n/16}\) decreases after 8, and \(e^{10}>2^{14}>(16/3)\sqrt{160}\). Consequently

\[|I_n/G_n-1|\leq\frac{21}{4n}\qquad(n\geq160).\]

The normalization is retained exactly. Equation (82) gives \(C_M/B_M=\sqrt{1+1/n}\,I_n/G_n\). Hence

\[|C_M/B_M-1| \leq\frac{23}{4n}+\frac{21}{8n^2}\leq\frac6n \qquad(n\geq160).\]

The final inequality only requires \(n\geq21/2\), and the stated domain is therefore sufficient. This proves the all-tail claim and its one-percent threshold.

Evaluation, replay and limits

The standard-library tool tools.risk_research.vertex_large_dimension_refined keeps the exact normalization and elementary logarithm, pi and integer-square-root enclosures. For \(4\leq M<162\), the same proof gives a valid fallback. With \(n=M-2\), put

\[E_n=\frac{17}{4n}+4\sqrt n(15/16)^{n-2} +\frac8{3\sqrt n}(16/17)^n.\]

Then \((1+1/(2n))E_n+1/(2n)\) bounds the relative error; here \(e^{-1/16}<16/17\). The fallback can exceed one and the lower constant endpoint is clipped to zero. The simple radius is used only on its proved domain. The threshold routine returns \(\max(162,2+\lceil6/\varepsilon\rceil)\) for positive rational target \(\varepsilon\), valid at every larger integer vertex count.

$ .venv/bin/python -m tools.risk_research.vertex_large_dimension_refined --vertices 602 --bits 47 --relative-target 1/100 --output .research/skeleton-risk/refined602.json
$ .venv/bin/python -m tools.risk_research.vertex_large_dimension_refined --verify .research/skeleton-risk/refined602.json

--verify accepts no generation overrides. It restores recorded precision, target and optional Arb/integral flags, recomputes all fields, and compares JSON scalar types strictly. Duplicate keys, extra fields, changed proof/source fingerprints and altered intervals are rejected. Fingerprints canonicalize CRLF to LF; the calculation guard compares raw source bytes before replacing output. Failed generation preserves an existing output, and verification preserves its input. Generation rejects output paths resolving to any evaluator or proof source, including symbolic-link aliases, before calculation. After calculation, the output’s parent directory is resolved and pinned, and the source check is repeated on that destination before writing. The final name stays unresolved, retaining atomic replacement of a leaf symbolic link while preserving its target.

Optional --with-arb --integral-check restores the scale and calls the existing positive-integral implementation without using the refined error bound. Arb precision is restored and saved balls round outward. Exact-series and positive-integral finite comparisons check normalization and implementation independently; the theorem above supplies the infinite-tail guarantee. Neither these checks nor the improved error assert a higher-order expansion or a simultaneous growing-degree limit.
