Single and assembled domains

The same model and fitting API handles simplices, products and glued cells:

from torch_bsf import BezierSpace, Samples, SimploidalSet, fit
from torch_bsf.sampling import sample

triangle_space = BezierSpace.simplex(2, degree=3)
prism_space = BezierSpace.simploid((2, 1), degree=(3, 2))

domain = SimploidalSet()
triangle = domain.add_simplex(2)
square = domain.add_simploid((1, 1))
domain.identify(triangle.face(0, 0), square.face(0, 0))
space = BezierSpace.on_set(domain, degree=2)
t1 = sample(triangle_space, 30, seed=1).parameters
t2 = sample(BezierSpace.simploid((1, 1), degree=2), 30, seed=2).parameters
observations = Samples.from_cells(space, [
    (triangle, t1, t1[:, :1]), (square, t2, t2[:, :1])])
result = fit(space, observations)

Shared face coefficients are stored once and ensure C0 continuity. The space snapshots the topology builder; subsequent builder mutations do not alter it. space.cell_id(handle) resolves original builder handles to stable IDs. space.control_index(index, cell=handle) resolves local indices to shared rows.

Points(parameters, cell_ids) preserves mixed-cell prediction order. Coordinates are padded to space.width; only each cell’s active coordinates are used. Each simplex factor has nonnegative barycentric coordinates summing to one. Saved models preserve stable IDs rather than mutable builder handles.

Homology

The topology of a simploidal set can be checked after it is built by computing its homology (Section 4.4 of Peltier and Lienhardt [PL18]). The chain group \(C_k\) is spanned by the non-degenerate simploids of dimension \(k\), and the boundary of a simploid \(\sigma\) of type \((a_1, \ldots, a_n)\) follows the sign rule of a product of simplices:

\[\partial \sigma = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=0}^{a_i} (-1)^{a_1 + \cdots + a_{i-1} + j} d^i_j \sigma,\]

where the terms whose face \(d^i_j \sigma\) is degenerate are dropped.

  • boundary_matrix() returns the integer matrix of \(\partial_k\), with rows simploids(k - 1) and columns simploids(k); it satisfies boundary_matrix(k) @ boundary_matrix(k + 1) == 0.

  • betti_numbers() returns the Betti numbers \(\beta_0, \ldots, \beta_{\dim}\), the ranks of the homology groups. The ranks are computed exactly by integer elimination, over the rationals (field="Q", default) or over \(\mathbb Z / 2\) (field="Z2"); the two differ only for spaces whose integral homology has torsion, such as non-orientable surfaces.

from torch_bsf.geometry import SimploidalSet

# A torus: a square whose opposite edges are identified
torus = SimploidalSet()
square = torus.add_simploid((1, 1))
torus.identify(square.face(0, 0), square.face(0, 1))
torus.identify(square.face(1, 0), square.face(1, 1))
print(torus.betti_numbers())

# A sphere: a square whose two edges face(1, *) are collapsed into
# vertices and whose two other edges are identified
sphere = SimploidalSet()
square = sphere.add_simploid((1, 1))
for j in range(2):
    edge = square.face(1, j)
    sphere.degenerate(edge, edge.face(0, 0), 0, -1)
sphere.identify(square.face(0, 0), square.face(0, 1))
print(sphere.betti_numbers())

# A projective plane: two triangles glued along their edges
rp2 = SimploidalSet()
t1, t2 = rp2.add_simplex(2), rp2.add_simplex(2)
rp2.identify(t1.face(0, 0), t2.face(0, 0))
rp2.identify(t1.face(0, 1), t2.face(0, 2))
rp2.identify(t1.face(0, 2), t2.face(0, 1))
print(rp2.betti_numbers(), rp2.betti_numbers(field="Z2"))

Use Points.from_cells(space, [(cell, parameters), ...]) to pack mixed-cell prediction batches without padding. Multi-cell models require explicit Points and never infer a cell from an untagged tensor.
